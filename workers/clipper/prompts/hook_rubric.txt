================================================================================
WHAT A HOOK IS
================================================================================

A hook is the first thing a viewer hears that makes stopping the scroll feel
involuntary. It must be one of:

  QUESTION        A direct question to the viewer. Strongest when it implies
                  the viewer is currently getting something wrong.
                  "మీ budget లో ఇది వస్తుందా?"

  BOLD CLAIM      A confident, contestable assertion. If nobody could disagree
                  with it, it is not a claim -- it is a description.
                  "ఈ area లో ఇంతకంటే మంచి deal దొరకదు."

  SPECIFIC NUMBER A precise figure, price, measurement, or date. Precision is
                  what sells it: "3.2 crore" hooks, "కొంచెం costly" does not.

  CONTRADICTION   Something that cuts against what the viewer expects, or
                  against what the speaker just said.
                  "అందరూ Gachibowli అంటారు. నేను చెప్పను."

  EMOTIONAL       Visible stakes: regret, fear of loss, pride, frustration.
  TENSION         Must be felt in the words, not merely a topic that is
                  emotional in the abstract.

NOT hooks: greetings, channel intros, "ఈ రోజు నేను చెప్పబోతున్నాను...",
throat-clearing, restating the video title, thanking the audience, generic
scene-setting.

================================================================================
SCORING
================================================================================

hook_score (0-10) -- how hard the opening line stops the scroll.

  9-10  Immediate. Question or number so specific the viewer needs the answer.
  7-8   Strong claim or real tension; a viewer would give it three seconds.
  5-6   Some interest, but generic. Could open a hundred other videos.
  3-4   Topic is relevant, opening line is flat.
  0-2   Throat-clearing, intro, or mid-sentence with no hook at all.

payoff_score (0-10) -- does the passage deliver what the hook promises?

  9-10  Question asked and answered, or claim made and evidenced, inside the clip.
  7-8   Mostly resolved; a small gap the viewer can fill.
  5-6   Partially resolved, or resolved vaguely.
  3-4   Sets up something it never delivers.
  0-2   Pure setup, or the payoff clearly lands outside this passage.

standalone_score (0-10) -- does it make sense with ZERO context?

  Test it literally: a stranger opens this clip having seen nothing else.

  9-10  Fully self-contained. No unexplained references.
  7-8   One mild unexplained reference that context makes obvious.
  5-6   Needs a little prior knowledge ("this project", "as I said").
  3-4   Depends on an earlier segment to make sense.
  0-2   Incomprehensible alone -- opens mid-argument or mid-list.

Penalise heavily in standalone_score: unexplained pronouns for people or
places, "ఇక్కడ" / "ఈ project" with no antecedent, mid-list items ("రెండోది..."),
and callbacks to earlier parts of the video.


================================================================================
CLIP FORMATS
================================================================================

A clip is not always one continuous window. Choose the shape that fits the
material, the way an editor would. The formats below are the only valid values
for `format`.

=== FORMATS ===
single_take: One unbroken segment that already works as-is. The default, and the right answer whenever the moment stands on its own. Do not manufacture complexity.
setup_payoff: A hook from one place joined to the segment it sets up. Use when the strongest opening line and the thing it promises are far apart in the source.
compilation: Three or more scattered spans on a single theme, presented as a list ("three mistakes", "every red flag"). Each span must make its own point; give each one a label.
contradiction: Two spans placed together because the second cuts against the first. The tension IS the clip; order matters more than proximity.
=== END FORMATS ===

Rules that apply to every format:

  * EXACTLY ONE span has role "hook", and it must be FIRST in playback order.
    Every format has to earn attention in the first two seconds.
  * Spans are listed in PLAYBACK order, which need not be source order.
  * Roles: "hook" | "body" | "point" | "payoff". Use "point" for the items of a
    compilation, "payoff" for the span that resolves a setup.
  * `label` is for `compilation` points ONLY -- spans whose role is "point".
    For EVERY other span, and for EVERY other format, `label` MUST be null.
    This is not cosmetic: a label renders a title overlay on the seam before
    that span. Labelling a hook, a body or a payoff puts a caption on screen
    that belongs to nothing, and the clip reads as mislabelled. If you are not
    building a numbered list, every label is null.
  * `theme` is one line saying what the clip is ABOUT. For a compilation it is
    what the points have in common.
  * Every span must be at least 3 seconds. Total 20-90 seconds.
  * Prefer single_take. Reach for a multi-span format only when the assembled
    version is genuinely better than the best continuous window.

coherence_score (0-10) -- does the assembled clip hold together as ONE piece?

  9-10  Reads as though it was shot this way. No seam a viewer would notice.
  7-8   Clearly assembled, but the logic carries it.
  5-6   The spans relate, but the jumps are felt.
  3-4   Feels like unrelated clips stapled together.
  0-2   Incoherent.

  For single_take this is simply how well the segment holds together.

================================================================================
WHAT TO RETURN
================================================================================

format                 One of the formats declared above.
theme                  One line: what this clip is ABOUT.
spans                  The spans in PLAYBACK order. Each has start_word_i and
                       end_word_i (global indices, copied from the [n] markers),
                       a role, and an optional label.
                       The first span must be the hook, and its start_word_i
                       must be the first word of a sentence -- never mid-phrase.
reason                 One or two sentences: which hook type it is, and why the
                       standalone score is what it is. Be specific and blunt --
                       this text is read when deciding whether to trust the pick.
suggested_title        A short shareable title in the same code-mixed register
                       as the speaker. Not a summary.

Be a harsh grader. Most passages in any video are ordinary; a rubric that
awards 7s to everything is useless for ranking. Reserve 9-10 for genuinely
exceptional moments, and do not inflate scores to be encouraging.
